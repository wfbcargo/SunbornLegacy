# Sunborn Legacy

*A living-world MMO about caravans, industry, and trade in a multiverse being endlessly reshaped.*

---

## The World

There is one sun. It burns in every world of the multiverse at once, and everything
that exists turns around it — planets, moons, gods. Divinity here isn't metaphor: the
celestial bodies **are** the divine beings, and chief among them is the Sun God.

Humanity is scattered across these worlds, most civilizations never learning how far
their species has spread. The flora and fauna drink the sun's power directly — flowers
bloom wildly, ethereal projections drift through the canopy, life is saturated with
light. Humanity cannot. Denied the gift, people build machines: solar plates,
generators, factories. **Industry is humanity's answer to divinity.**

Except, rarely, a child is born **Sunborn** — able to manifest the Sun God's power
directly. They endure heat and cold that would kill anyone else, and their gifts
surface in wildly different shapes: mages, druids, shadows, necromancers, demons.

And the worlds do not sit still. Seasons swing between flood and drought. Quakes raise
mountains and carve new rivers. Volcanoes drown valleys in lava that cools to black
glass and leaves soil richer than anything that came before. And every so often a
world's solar moon focuses the sun's light into a beam that walks slowly across the
surface, melting sand to lava and boiling shallow seas away.

The Sun God is not destroying his worlds. He is **shaping** them — and it is said he
is watching to see which of his Sunborn are worthy of ascension.

**The world you build on will not be the world you leave.**

---

## The Pitch

You don't play a hero. You manage people — a roster of characters organized into
**caravans**, each one a mobile factory crawling across a vast hex world that is
genuinely alive and genuinely changing. Islands erode into ocean. Forests dry into
savanna. Sand struck by the beam melts and cools into fields of black glass, and a
quake a decade later shatters that glass back into sand. Every world runs its own set
of cycles — seasons, quakes, eruptions, the beam — and any hex can eventually become
any other, given enough time and the right events. The map isn't scenery; it's the
supply side of the economy.

Materials are **regional**, so nobody is self-sufficient. Anything worth building
needs inputs from somewhere else — sometimes from another world entirely, reachable
only through rituals like dying inside an abyssal aura, or stepping through a rift
built by an alcove of druids. Distance is the price mechanism, and freight is paid
in real time — the one currency nobody can inflate.

There is **no exponential power curve**. A veteran isn't a thousand times stronger;
they're better established, better connected, and know more of the map. So a brand
new player sitting on the only glass flats for four hundred tiles is immediately,
genuinely important to everyone around them.

Then the world changes under you — and it takes the cities with it. Which is the
point. You can't hold ground forever, only decide what's worth carrying forward. And
the land the beam ruins today is the volcanic soil someone farms in a decade.

## Who It's For

- **Factorio players** who want the "what can I make next" curve without the solitude
- **EVE admirers** who love a real player-driven economy but not the combat commitment
- **Idle/incremental players** tired of games that solve themselves and end in zeroes
- **Programmers** — the entire game is a public API and MCP surface. Write bots that
  run your caravans. *Screeps* proved this audience exists and stays for a decade.
- **GMs, DMs, and server operators** — people who want to *run* a world, not just
  live in one
- **Solarpunk and cozy-apocalypse readers** — warmth, overgrowth, and a clock overhead

The center of gravity is the overlap: **systems thinkers and world runners.**

## How It's Played

**Pace.** The world moves in 6-second ticks. A tile takes minutes to cross; a world
takes real days. Check in a few times a day. Nothing ever demands reflexes — the
clock is always the bottleneck, never your hands.

**The loop.**
1. **Move** — caravans crawl the hex map. A caravan travels at the speed of its
   slowest member.
2. **Build** — outfit vehicles with mounts, wheels, and stations: grow food, collect
   water, charge solar batteries, craft components.
3. **Consume** — characters eat, machines need fuel and ingredients. Nothing is free.
4. **Settle** — found outposts and cities where the materials are.
5. **Trade** — build markets, haul freight, work regional price gaps. Goods
   physically move; nothing teleports.
6. **Reach further** — discover the rituals that open other worlds.
7. **Ride the cycles** — read the seasons, quakes, eruptions and the beam. Evacuate,
   salvage, and rebuild where the land is new. That's the Legacy.

**Three ways to play.**
- **By hand**, in a client built to be fun on its own terms.
- **By script** — every action is a public API call, and third-party clients are
  encouraged, not tolerated. Build a better UI than the official one; that's the goal.
- **By running a world** — become a **Game Master**. Choose your world's cycles and
  their severity, spawn events and NPCs, decide how it connects to the multiverse, and
  advertise its character and open slots. Run it forever, or as a finite campaign with
  an ending.

Every NPC belongs to an account — even when that account is a program on a remote
server. There is no separate NPC system. The world's population and its players are
the same kind of thing.
